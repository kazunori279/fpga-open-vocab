# History, frictions and what they taught

Everything the README used to carry about *how the board got here* rather than
what it is. Three kinds of thing live here:

- **the timeline** — every milestone, what it was scoped to do, and what it
  actually measured;
- **the frictions** — the hardware questions that had to be answered before
  anything could be built, several of which were answered wrongly first;
- **the learnings** — the transferable rules the measurements produced, which
  are the reason this file exists rather than being deleted.

This file is **curated and edited**: when a claim in it is overturned the
overturning goes in beside it, and the numbers are kept current where they are
load-bearing. That is what distinguishes it from the two raw logs it sits on
top of, which are **append-only** and are never edited after an entry closes:

- [`milestones.md`](milestones.md) — the milestone-by-milestone record, M0
  through M21, in full detail;
- [`bring-up-log.md`](bring-up-log.md) — dated bench entries, newest first.

For what the board *is*, read [`../README.md`](../README.md) and then
[`architecture.md`](architecture.md).

## Status and roadmap

Two of the milestones below were scoped as **GO/NO-GO gates** — measurements that, had they come out badly, would have ended the project rather than redirected it. M2 asked whether the on-board link is fast and clean enough to be worth using at all; M4 asked whether a model small enough to fit can still tell the queries apart. **Both are passed, and the whole appliance runs on the board**: 174 blocks, all 8 conv layers on the tile, 512 of 512 embedding floats exact, a live camera, and a query typed on the host — a whole camera frame in **363 ms** at the shipped 320/160 — **373 ms** by the board's own wall clock — **346 ms** of which is the encoder, and 11.33× the MCU alone at the clock that ratio was measured at. What is left is not speed. It is the decision rule that turns 512 floats into an answer: [M20](milestones.md#m20--gate-on-presence-rank-the-states--2026-08-10-shipped-and-its-premise-is-falsified-on-hardware--161-against-794-for-the-rule-it-replaced) is where that stopped being assumed to be easy, and [M21](milestones.md#m21--learn-the-reference-and-put-two-edges-on-the-presence-stage--2026-08-11-120120-held-out-on-the-board-against-90180-for-ranking-the-same-frames) is where the board stopped being told a threshold and started being **shown** the scene — **120/120 held out, on a run where ranking the same frames scores 50.0%**.

Every "x the MCU" below is against `encoder_fast` — the same model running on the MCU alone, hand-tuned, with no FPGA involved — **measured in the same boot**, not against a remembered constant. It was 3,507 ms at M7c and then exactly 3,448 in each of M7f, M7g and M7h; M7i's epilogue took it to **3,359**, which is why a 7.2% faster frame moved the ratio only 4.8%. M5b's tuned figure, measured before that epilogue existed, is 3,358. Every frame figure is bit-exact against `firmware/encoder.c`. The full record, including the projections that were wrong and why, is in [`milestones.md`](milestones.md).

| ✅ | **after M21** the trade the overlap made had never been priced | The row above traded freshness for throughput and quoted only the half that improved, because **nothing in the tree measured latency at all**. `cam.c` now latches every trigger's timestamp and `m9` prints the frame's age at the instant the LED changes, which turns the trade into two numbers instead of one. [#14](https://github.com/kazunori279/fpga-open-vocab/issues/14) |

**851 ms was called the floor for this board, and it is now 304.** The claim rested on 313 ms of it being the tile computing at a fabric that caps at 125 MHz. Both halves of that turned out to be moveable, in opposite ways: M16 did not clock the tile faster, it gave the tile less to do, pairing kernel taps so nine sweeps become six — and then the clock push did exactly the thing M16 had ruled out, because the tile is clocked *by* the link and `sys_clk` reaches it after all — twice, because the second clock audit found 280 MHz above the 220 the first one had stopped at. `RUN` is 118 ms now. What remains is [the optimization ladder](milestones.md#the-road-to-280-ms) as a record of where it stops. ~~And the next rung is firmware rather than fabric — core 1's weight builds are 193 ms against a 387 ms frame.~~ **M17 measured that and it was wrong**: core 1 is only 56% busy, so those builds are worth about 20 ms of frame, not their face value. `RUN` is still the largest line and it is still fabric.

**The last five milestones — M7d, M7e, M7f, M7h, M10 — share a pattern worth carrying forward:** each one predicted a component saving correctly and then collected less than face value at the frame, by a different mechanism every time. M7d left 563 ms of CPU outside the wire it thought it had covered; M7e projected 380 ms from the second core and got 193, because work already hidden in a DMA window costs nothing whichever core runs it; M7f-1 moved 157 ms off core 0 and bought 59, with 101 coming back as stall behind one FIFO; the jumper saved 286 ms of *wire* and **0 ms of frame** until M7g-2 found the three reasons and recovered 165; M7h removed 211 ms of CPU for 58 ms of frame; and M10 was scoped around ~450 ms that measured 32. The component arithmetic has been right nearly every time. **What has been wrong, repeatedly, is the assumption that a component and the frame are the same thing** — which is why nothing here is claimed until it has been measured in the same boot as its control.

**M7i is the first one to convert at nearly face value, and the reason is the exception that proves the rule.** All 84 ms of it came out of `W1_LO` — `W1_HI` measured 292 → 293 ms and the DRAIN decode inside `W1_LO` measured 159 both times, so `scatter` alone went **141 → 57 ms** — and `W1_LO` is the queue whose overruns *become* core 0's stall instead of hiding in the wire's shadow. The stall fell 127 → 66 ms and the frame moved 66 of the 84, a 79% conversion against M7h's 40%. The same 84 ms taken out of `W1_HI` would have hidden in the wire and collected far less.

**M16 converted at 100% and 66% simultaneously, on the same board in the same run, which sharpens the rule rather than breaking it.** Config A turned 93 ms of `RUN` into 94 ms of frame; config C turned 94 into 62. Nothing about the saving differed — the tile ran the identical 16.30 Mclk in both — so what differed is what was standing behind it. Config C's wire is three times as fast, so it is the configuration that runs out of shadow first, and core 1's weight builds stepped into the light: same `WGT` cycles, 52% more wall time. **A component saving is worth face value only until it uncovers the next thing**, and which configuration uncovers it first is a property of the machine, not of the saving.

**And then the clock push broke the pattern by going the other way, which is its own lesson.** It converted at 100% — 182 ms of components, 182 ms of frame — because it was not a component saving at all; it scaled everything at once, so there was nothing left standing behind it to uncover. The transferable part is not the number. It is *why the number was available for three milestones and nobody took it*: every sweep here descends from 150 MHz and stops at the first rung that passes, so the logs said "bit-exact at 75.0 MHz" and were read as "bit-exact **up to** 75.0 MHz". **A ladder that only steps down cannot find a ceiling; it can only confirm the rung it starts on.** Two milestones' worth of plans were written around a limit that had never been measured, including M16's own statement that `RUN` "is the one line no link change can touch". Worth auditing any other place where a measured *pass* has been quietly read as a measured *bound*.

**That audit was run, and the first thing it caught was the sentence above.** 220 MHz had become the new top row within one milestone of the rule being written down, and three more table entries found **280 / 140** — another 84 ms, from a change with no RTL in it. Two things came out of it beyond the number. One: the prediction written into the code before the run — "the MCU will be fine and the Trion will be what stops" — was wrong in the interesting half. Nothing stopped, and the rungs that failed failed on the *MCU* side. The device assumed to be the limit has now not been the limit twice.

Two: **that row's own explanation was the next thing to fall.** It said 260 failed because the SDK's clock search hands it the highest VCO that produces the rate, and 260's lands near the PLL ceiling — a clean story, published with its falsifiable half marked as untested. Forcing 260 through the half-rate VCO instead was then measured, and **it fails identically**. Filling the sweep in at 4 MHz showed why: not a rung but a **contiguous dead band, 124–130 MHz link**, with 264 passing at a *higher* VCO than the one that had been blamed — a refutation that was sitting in the original table next to 176 MHz, unchecked. **A hole in a coarse sweep is not a hole until the sweep is fine enough to see its edges; measure the width before explaining the cause.** The band tracks `link_clk` and nothing else, which points at sampling phase rather than any margin.

**That hypothesis was then tested too, and this time it survived with numbers attached.** A uniform 4 MHz grid from 76 to 400 MHz found **two** bands, not one — link 39–41 and 122–130 — spaced 86 MHz, which is a round-trip delay of **τ = 11.7 ns**. Their widths (2.5 and 8–10 MHz, the narrow one re-swept at 1 MHz resolution) grow linearly with frequency, as a fixed setup-and-hold window must, and solving each band separately for that window gives **0.73 ns and 0.83 ns** — two independent measurements 13% apart. Sweeping core voltage moves the band **+1 MHz per 0.05 V**, upward, exactly as faster silicon and a smaller τ require; cold versus warm shows nothing. **260 MHz passes at 1.20 V**, twelve for twelve, through the very VCO that was originally blamed — the rate was never broken, it just sits in a band whose position the firmware picks when it picks a voltage. The shipped 140 MHz link would need **+0.45 V** to be reached by that band, and the sanctioned ceiling is 0.05 V away.

And the bound the row above declined to guess at: **344 MHz sys / 172 MHz link is bit-exact, three boots — the Trion at 2.65× its signed-off Fmax — and 348 hangs the MCU** with no `link error` printed, so it is the core or the flash and not the fabric. 344 has zero margin and is a bound, not an operating point.

**What an operating point would cost was then measured, because the firmware's voltage thresholds turned out to be invented rather than measured.** Everything above 280 had been run at 1.30 V on the strength of a constant this repo wrote to keep the MCU out of a question about the Trion — generous on purpose, and the wrong thing to inherit into a shipping rate. Sweeping 276–344 at every rail, three boots each: bit-exact to **344 at 1.30 V, 340 at 1.25, 332 at 1.20, 312 at 1.15**, with 1.10 excluded by monotonicity. **Not one non-exact transaction at any rail** — every failure up there is the core going quiet, never the link or the tile. The shape is the point: the top three rails are worth 4 and 8 MHz, dropping from 1.20 to 1.15 costs **20**, so the ceiling saturates and the first half of the sweep — three points on the flat part — was written up as "barely a function of the rail" before the bottom half proved otherwise. The consequence is concrete: **320 MHz sys / 160 MHz link needs 1.20 V, which is two steps below `VREG_VOLTAGE_MAX`**, with 12 MHz of clean margin under it and all of 1.25 V in reserve — so an elevated operating point would not mean an appliance parked at the regulator's ceiling. It is still `m6` evidence only: one block, 4 ms, one core, XIP flash. Picking the rate is an `m7` question and `m7`'s own ladder still stops at 280.

One rule needed a third branch. A printed `link error` means the MCU is alive; silence had meant the core or the flash. **336 MHz at 1.20 V is neither** — three consecutive `printf`s vanished and the board then printed the *next* rate correctly, in two boots of three. **A missing row is not proof of a stopped board;** only silence that never ends, with the board still enumerated and deaf to both recovery paths, is the wedge.

**M17 then found the rule's mirror, which is the more dangerous half.** *A component cost is worth its face value only if something is actually waiting on it.* Core 1 spends 82 ms in `gb_weights()`, but it is only 56% busy, so core 0 waits about 20 ms for it — and the way to see that was not a profiler. It was the `ns/clk` column, printed since M7 as a link-health readout: `DRAIN`, `RUN` and `ACT` all agree on 1.2–1.4 µs of fixed per-transaction framing, `WGT` carries 12.2 µs, and the difference times 1856 transactions *is* the wait. **Two milestones were planned against a cost that nothing was blocked on.** The proximate cause is smaller and more embarrassing than the lesson: `W1_HI` runs two callbacks and they summed into one counter, so both M14 and M17 correctly measured a number that was not the number they thought. Audit shared accumulators before believing a component is immovable.

## Why this shape

This section is the feasibility pass that chose the architecture above, kept in its original form because the alternatives it rejected keep looking attractive again later. Where a later measurement contradicts it, the original text stays and a **superseded** note follows it — the gap between what was predicted and what was measured is the part worth keeping. For the current numbers, read [Architecture](architecture.md); for how each one was arrived at, [`milestones.md`](milestones.md). The design-time link and frame model that preceded all of the measurements is kept there too, as [an appendix](milestones.md#appendix-the-design-time-performance-model) — none of its rows survived contact with the board.

### Resource budget

| Resource | Amount |
|---|---|
| T8 logic | 7,384 LE, **8 × 18×18 multipliers** |
| T8 on-chip RAM | **123 Kbit = 15.4 KB** |
| RP2354A SRAM | 520 KB |
| Weight storage | **2 MB stacked flash** today. Budget after M14 halved the weight width: 768 KB int4 weights + 173 KB T8 bitstream + ~60 KB firmware = **1.00 MB of 2.10 MB** (it was 1.65 MB at int8). U1's 2 MB of PSRAM is fitted but accidental and untested by the vendor, so it is not headroom we can count on ([#1](#verify-before-building)). Budget against flash alone |
| RP2354A GPIO | 30 total, **6 on the header**, 13 unbonded |
| MCU↔FPGA link | **3 contiguous bits** (GPIO1–3) + 1 flag |

Realized compute: **8 hard multipliers, not 16.** An earlier draft of this table claimed they pack 2× for int8; they do not. Two int8×int8 products sharing an operand are 16 bits wide each, and 18 bits cannot give 16 bits of field separation, so one 18×18 holds one int8 MAC. [M6](milestones.md#m6--fpga-gemm-tile--bit-exact-on-silicon-2048-of-2048-accumulators) built the tile and the Efinity report agrees: **8 / 8 multipliers, 100%.**

8 hard MACs at the 75 MHz [M6c measured](milestones.md#m6c--on-board-2048-of-2048-at-every-rate) is 600 MMAC/s of *fabric* capability. That is not the binding number — the link is — but it is the honest one.

### Rejected: stream a real CLIP from external memory

Weight streaming needs weight *reuse* per fetch, and reuse is capped by how many tokens fit on-chip. With 15.4 KB of BRAM and a 384-dim activation you hold ~20 tokens, so a 196-token ViT re-streams the full weight matrix **~10× per frame**.

[MobileCLIP-S0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17049) (11.4M params / 1.5 GMACs) → 11 MB int8 × 10 = **114 MB/frame**. At ~30 MB/s that's 4 s/frame, and 11 MB doesn't fit in 4 MB of onboard storage anyway — add an SD card at ~10 MB/s and it's 10–30 s/frame. Dead.

**The T8's tiny BRAM is the binding constraint, not its compute.** Distilling a small student that fits resident is the only version that works.

### Rejected: FPGA-alone with HyperRAM

The netlist makes this **less** hopeless than first written: there are 18 FPGA header pins, not 11, and 7 of them sit in bank 2A, so DQ[7:0] straddles only two banks rather than three or four. The remaining objections still hold, though: DDR gives a ~5 ns window across breadboard jumpers with 10 kΩ pull-ups hanging on the config-strap nets; a HyperBus controller costs 500–1,200 LE competing with the MAC array; and Efinix's [HyperRAM](https://www.infineon.com/products/memories/psram-pseudostatic-dram) IP targets T20-and-up. If the FPGA-alone path is ever revisited, use **two APS6404 QSPI PSRAMs in parallel** (4 bits each, SDR at 100 MHz = 100 MB/s, ~300 LE controller) instead — and note that the MCU-side pin famine that kills the hybrid dataplane does not apply here, which makes this the strongest of the rejected options.

> **Promoted to [M10](milestones.md#m10--take-the-tile-off-the-links-clock--closed-measured-70-mhz-and-the-prize-is-32-ms), 2026-07-30 — and demoted again 2026-07-31.** The APS6404 line above stopped being a footnote once M7c costed the whole frame. The MCU is the tile's only clock, so RUN — the MCU shifting idle bytes so the multipliers can advance — is 314 ms that no firmware can reach, and the software ladder bottomed out at 917 ms against a 265 ms MAC floor. PSRAM on the T8's own bus would take WGT, ACT, DRAIN *and* RUN off the link at once, leaving ~49 KB per frame.
>
> **It is back to rejected, for a duller reason than any above: the part is not obtainable.** No off-the-shelf QSPI PSRAM breakout was findable, and this project cannot fabricate a board — so the arithmetic never got tested. **The onboard U1 is not a substitute** — it hangs off the MCU's QMI, so anything stored there still crosses the link, which is the whole cost being deleted.
>
> **And the surviving half died too, one day later.** Giving the tile the T8's own clock does not turn RUN's 314 ms into 0; RUN's idle bytes *are* the tile's cycle count (`m7.c:490`), so a second clock only helps in proportion to how much faster the tile runs on it. Synthesized standalone it reaches **70 MHz**, capped by a memory-block-to-multiplier path at Logic Level 0. Worth ~32 ms. M10 is closed; the paragraph above is left as written because the reasoning that promoted it was sound and only the unmeasured number was missing.

### Rejected: USB webcam, DVP camera

RP2354 USB is 1.1 full-speed (12 Mbps); UVC webcams are all USB 2.0 high-speed. DVP needs ~14 pins (8 data + PCLK/HREF/VSYNC/XCLK + SCCB) and the header can't spare them. The T8 also has **no MIPI D-PHY** (starts at T13/T20). ArduCam SPI with an onboard FIFO is the only shape that fits.

### Tier 3 vs Tier 2 — revisited after the netlist

The original feasibility pass compared three tiers and picked **Tier 3** (MCU owns the weights, FPGA is a GEMM coprocessor) because the 8-bit PIO bus at ~70 MB/s beat the 4-bit QSPI PSRAM at ~45 MB/s. **That comparison was decided by a number that turned out to be wrong by 3–9×.** Redone with the measured topology, taking the better of the two link configurations:

| | **Tier 3** MCU-side weights | **Tier 2** FPGA + external QSPI PSRAM |
|---|---|---|
| Weight store | RP2354A **2 MB stacked flash**, MCU-side *(U1's 2 MB PSRAM sits behind the same QMI and would add headroom, not bandwidth — [#1](#verify-before-building))* | APS6404, **8 MB**, FPGA-side *(~$1, breadboard)* |
| Max int8 params | ~1.5 M | **~6 M** |
| Path the model data crosses | 3-bit on-board link, **26.8 out / 8.9 back MB/s** | 4-bit QSPI @ 100 MHz SDR, **50 MB/s** |
| Extra pins | one jumper | 6 of the 12 strap-free FPGA header pins |
| Per-frame traffic over that path | 3.5 MB out + 2 MB back | image in + embedding out = **~49 KB** |
| Memory time | **225 ms** *(return-bound)* | ~110 ms |
| Compute (250 MMAC @ 3–4 GMAC/s) | ~70 ms *(overlaps)* | ~70 ms *(overlaps)* |
| **Frame time** | **~230 ms** | **~130–180 ms** |
| vs MCU-only *(measured 3.36 s, [M5b](milestones.md#m5b--tuned-mcu-baseline--3358-msframe-bit-exact-74-the-reference))* | ~15× | **~19–26×** |
| vs MCU-only *(~1.7 s, the original estimate)* | ~7× | ~10× |
| Bottleneck | the 1-bit **return** path | PSRAM bandwidth |
| Non-GEMM ops (norm, activation, pool) | MCU, in C | **FPGA, in RTL** |
| Build cost | one jumper — otherwise this works today | PSRAM controller (~300 LE) **plus the entire encoder in RTL** |

Tier 2 still wins, but by far less than it did an hour ago, and the reason is not that Tier 2's memory got faster — 50 MB/s is the same number as before. It is that Tier 2 **never touches the link**: once the weights and activations live on the FPGA's own bus, the link carries a 48 KB image in and a 512-d embedding out, which is a rounding error at any of these rates.

> **Widened since, and it was the argument for [M10](milestones.md#m10--take-the-tile-off-the-links-clock--closed-measured-70-mhz-and-the-prize-is-32-ms) until M10 closed.** Tier 3's "per-frame traffic" row above says 3.5 MB out + 2 MB back; M7c's computed figure is **8.151 MB**, because the row omitted the RUN idle bytes — 2.78 MB of them. The row called those bytes "the MCU clocking the tile rather than data", which is true and was then read as *therefore removable*. **It is not.** The MCU clocks the tile because the tile has to be clocked; RUN's byte count is the sweep length (`m7.c:490`), so the bytes and the compute are the same 314 ms seen twice. Tier 3's memory time is 1,140 ms measured (M7f items 1 and 3) and 644 ms of wire after M7h, not 225 ms.
>
> **The two columns therefore stay an either/or.** M10 was supposed to buy Tier 2's decisive property — operands off the link — while keeping Tier 3's student, tile and bit-exact contract. It cannot: the operands cannot live FPGA-side without PSRAM that is not buyable, and 2026-07-31's Fmax measurement says the tile has no speed to give back either. Tier 2 remains a rewrite, not an upgrade.

**But the two halves of Tier 2 have to be bought together, and that is the catch:**

- *Weights on the FPGA, MCU still sequencing layers* — activations still cross the link, and the 2 MB coming back is exactly the part that cannot be widened: **≈ 225 ms**, i.e. no better than Tier 3, for the price of a memory controller. Not worth building.
- *Weights **and** activations on the FPGA, FPGA runs the whole encoder* — this is the 130–180 ms column. It requires layernorm, activation functions and pooling in RTL inside 7,384 LE, alongside the MAC array and the PSRAM controller. That is a far larger project than "GEMM tile plus a bus".

So Tier 2 is now clearly the better *architecture* and clearly the worse *next step*. The order that respects both:

1. **M2 measures the real link rate.** If configuration C measures near its ceiling, Tier 3 lands close enough to Tier 2 that none of this matters.
2. **M4 (the distillation gate) runs regardless** — it is host-only, and a student that cannot separate the queries kills both tiers equally. If a 1.5 M-param student passes, Tier 3's capacity ceiling is not binding and the 8 MB is worth less than it looks.
3. Only if M2 comes in well under the ceiling *and* M4 says the model needs more than 1.5 M params does Tier 2's cost become justified.

Tier 2b (HyperRAM, 8-bit DDR, 100–200 MB/s, ~80–110 ms/frame) stays rejected on the grounds in the next section — a ~5 ns DDR window across breadboard jumpers with pull-ups hanging on config-strap nets. It wants a real PCB.

**#1 was answered wrongly twice, in opposite directions, and the second answer was the worse one.** First it was "populated" because `bom_JLCPCB.csv` lists the part. Then `psram_detect_size()` returned 0, and the search for a reason found `dnp exclude_from_pos_files exclude_from_bom` on U1 in the `.kicad_pcb` plus its absence from `build/positions.csv` — which looked conclusive, since JLCPCB places from the CPL. So the answer flipped to "not populated", and the README, the pin map and the firmware were all edited to say so.

Then a photograph of the underside settled it in about five seconds. **U1 is soldered on.** So is **J3**, which carries the same `dnp` flags. The premise underneath the whole chain was false: *the exclusion flags in this repo do not describe the manufactured board.* `positions.csv` is generated from those same flags, so it was never independent evidence — it was the same claim, restated. The one real signal, `psram_detect_size() == 0`, is consistent with a chip that is present and not answering, and that is what it turned out to mean.

What generalizes is not "read the CPL instead of the BOM". It is that **a vendor design repo is a design source, not a build record**, and "is this part on the board" is a question about an object. Two pieces of physical-world evidence — the product page and the press coverage — were both pointing the right way and were argued down by file metadata. The correction cost one photograph, which should have been the first step rather than the last.

The blast radius was small only because M5 was written to fall back to flash XIP if U1 did not answer, so the bench trip still produced its number.

**#5 was the sneaky one and it bit.** RP2350 PIO `out`/`in` pin groups must be contiguous; the widest contiguous RP run wired to the FPGA is 3 bits (GPIO1–3), and no jumper can widen it because the GPIO that would bridge the header pairs are not bonded out. This is the single most consequential finding so far.

**A correction to the first reading of #5.** The original conclusion — "the link is 1 bit of data, because the clock and the return line eat two of the three" — was wrong, and wrong in a way worth recording. `out_base`, `in_base` and `sideset_base` are three independent PIO registers; contiguity is required only *within* a group. So the clock never had to live inside the contiguous run. One jumper from header pad PIN2 (GPIO22) to pad PIN17 puts it on FPGA ball **B3 = `GPIOL_16_CLK2`**, which is both the only global-clock ball the RP can reach and, conveniently, outside GPIO1–3. Forward data goes 1 bit → 3 bits. The "no jumper widens the contiguous run" finding stands; the inference drawn from it did not.

Exactly the BOM part, exactly the BOM density, parsing commands correctly. Successor to #1 and it closes the same way — by finding the chip healthier than the diagnostic claimed. Candidates (a) QPI, (b) sampling point, (c) pad isolation and (d) solder fault are all now dead: rev 2 swept SCK across 16× (25 MHz → 1.5 MHz) × `RXDELAY` 0–3 and got twenty byte-identical rows while every CS0 control row decoded, which no timing fault survives and no statistical fault reproduces. **What remains open is narrow: why 18 bit-times?** `0x9F` + 24-bit address puts MFID at bit 32; it arrives at 50. Not byte-aligned, not nibble-aligned, invariant under clock. Rev 3 measured rather than theorized and cleared three more candidates: the reply **repeats with a 64-bit period** (one whole ID record, streamed over and over), dead opcodes `0x00`/`0xFF` return nothing (so `0x9F` really is what U1 is answering), and the per-lane quad capture puts the data on **SD1** — the correct wire, no crosstalk ghost. Rev 4's 2×2 matrix (both code paths × both chip selects) then **exonerated the host**: `flash_do_cmd_cs` and `raw_xfer` both frame CS0 at bit 8 and both slip +18 on CS1. And board #1 — a different die, serial `…1a 54 5d` against board #2's `…19 78 30` — slips by the same 18, so it is not a bad part either. **Closing this as bounded:** chip, wire, opcode and host are all cleared, `9Fh` has no dummy cycles per the datasheet, and an 18-bit offset is reachable by no QMI register (`RXDELAY` moves within a bit; dummy settings move whole bytes). The next instrument is a scope at the package, and PSRAM is headroom rather than a dependency. **CLOSED 2026-07-30 by the vendor:** U1 was *not intended to be fitted* — accidentally assembled, left populated, and **never tested on any Forgix board**. There was no working configuration to find. Adiuvo confirms nothing in the schematic or layout explains the delay and is equally at a loss; the 18 bits stay unexplained, on a part this project does not use. See [the reply](bring-up-log.md#vendor-reply-2026-07-30--10-closed-u1-was-never-meant-to-be-there). |
| 11 | **Half of the z-score is learned on the bench and half is still COCO's. Does the other half need to be learned too?** | **Open, and deliberately deferred out of [M12](milestones.md#m12--contrast-queries-and-a-background-that-stops-moving--2026-08-08-the-first-state-question-answered-backwards-and-reproducibly), 2026-08-08.** `zscore()` is `(cos − qbg[i]) / qsd[i]`. M9 made the **mean** live — the board learns its own room, which is what killed the `laptop` false positive — and M12 froze it after a warm-up. The **scale** is still the spread of that query's COCO negatives, so the numerator is measured in this room and the denominator in someone else's dataset. A running variance would close the asymmetry, and the reason it is not built yet is that it does not obviously work: variance collapses toward 0 on a static scene, which is exactly the scene M12's hold is designed for, so z explodes — and the floor that fixes that becomes another constant tuned by eye on this bench, which is the failure mode this project keeps writing down. **The deferral was also methodological:** M12 changed the background policy and the query form in one milestone, and adding a third change to the same score would have made a bad bench result unattributable. Worth revisiting once there is a change/event formulation to test it against, since that is where a live scale would matter most. |

|---|---|---|
| **MCU↔FPGA link is only 3 bits wide** | **Realized, mitigated as far as it goes** *(was "non-contiguous GPIO", High)* | One jumper moves the clock to B3 so all 3 bits carry data, 1 → 3 bits forward. **M2 measured 8.94 MB/s on one line and predicted 26.8 with the jumper; [M7f-2](milestones.md#m7f-2--the-config-c-jumper-and-300-ms-that-arrived-as-zero) soldered it and measured 16.791 MB in 637 ms — 26.4 MB/s**, 1.6% under the prediction, bit-exact at 75 MHz. Real, and still a third of the original 8-bit hope. There is no fourth line: the run is exhausted |
| **The return path is 1 bit and cannot be widened** | **High** *(new, and it is now the binding constraint)* | GPIO5 is `CDONE`, GPIO7 has no pad — no contiguous neighbour exists. Design M6/M7 so results come back small: push whole layers, read one answer |

M6's in-fabric im2col cut the traffic that has to cross it from 9.9 to 3.57 MB/frame *forward* — but M7c costed the whole frame at **8.151 MB** including the RUN idle bytes and DRAIN that figure omitted, and the board then moved exactly that — **2,164 ms measured** in configuration A, 1,481 after M7d, 1,140 after M7e and M7f items 1 and 3, 975 after the jumper, and **917 ms after M7h**, 3.76× the MCU. **The jumper is now measured and it is worth what this row said it would be, but less than half of it arrived on the first try.** It cannot touch RUN or DRAIN, so it bought ACT + WGT 453 → 167 ms and a wire of 923 → 637; the frame moved 1,140 → 1,144 until [M7g-2](milestones.md#m7g-2--the-reference-the-hint-slots-and-the-word-loop--config-c-1144--975-ms) found the width-dependent reference bug that was eating it, and then 975. Of that, 313 is the tile computing and 66 is core 0 waiting on a queue. Honest fallback is still MCU-only inference |
| ~~1.5M-param student can't separate the queries~~ | **Retired** | M4 passed: 1.40 M params retain **94%** of the queries CLIP ViT-B/16 itself clears, and simulated int8 costs nothing. Residual worry is narrower — see the `person`/`chair` note in M4: the prompt ensemble, not the student, is the weak link on near-chance queries. **M18 acted on exactly that** and changed the teacher rather than the student: same 1.40 M parameters, a space that can tell an opened book from a closed one, 91% retention instead of 94% |

The residual bug is that its reply lands 18 bit-times out of frame — reproduced on **both boards**, on two different dies, and rev 4's 2×2 matrix clears our driver of causing it, so it is neither a dead part nor a host bug. Reported upstream; not a blocker. **Absorbed either way:** the 1.42 MB int8 blob fits the 2 MB stacked flash alongside the 173 KB bitstream, and M5 measured the encoder running from flash XIP with the fetch nowhere near the bottleneck. | **Core 1 buys a class of bug bit-exactness will not reliably catch** | **Realized, Low** *(taken on in [M7e](milestones.md#m7e--use-the-other-core--1485--1292-ms-firmware-only); the mitigation ran and passed)* | Every failure this project has had so far was deterministic: a wrong bit boundary or a wrong lane order returns the *same* wrong tensor every run, so one strap settles it. A missing `__dmb()` or a buffer published one index early does not — it returns the right tensor most of the time and a slightly wrong one under a timing that a `printf` can perturb. So "the embedding matched" stops being sufficient evidence. Mitigated by keeping every mode as one binary A/B'd in the same boot ([M5b's rule](bring-up-log.md#2026-07-30--the-tuned-baseline-and-a-28-error-we-nearly-shipped)), by running the per-block accumulator sweep rather than only the per-layer CRC, and by the fact that the handoff is single-producer/single-consumer over buffers M7d already doubled — the narrowest shape a concurrency bug can hide in. **All of that ran on 2026-07-31 and passed, twice**: four modes bit-exact for M7e, then six for M7f — the DRAIN decode and the priority split each adding a mode — with 174/174 blocks swept exact in each boot. The sweep was promoted from a build option to an untimed final pass precisely so it could not be a *different binary* — against this bug class, reflashing is itself a perturbation. One latent instance was still found, and by reading rather than by the board: block *b+2* reuses the `got[]` half block *b*'s scatter is reading, and the invariant that saves it holds only because `c1_scatter` is never set without `c1_build`. Now an explicit `w1_wait()` that costs nothing when the invariant already holds — and M7f's priority split then *deleted the invariant*, since order no longer holds between the two queues, so that defensive guard became the only thing keeping the reuse safe. Downgraded to Low because the surface is small and instrumented, not because it went away |

| **A milestone's key part turns out not to be buyable** | **Realized** *(new, 2026-07-31; the only risk on this table that is not technical)* | M10 was scoped around **two APS6404 QSPI PSRAMs on the T8's header** and the arithmetic was never the problem — 100 MB/s for ~300 LE. **No off-the-shelf QSPI PSRAM breakout was findable, and this project has no way to fabricate one**, so the ~280 ms row was withdrawn without ever being testable. The board's own U1 is not a substitute: it is on the *MCU's* QMI, so operands there still cross the link. The mitigation *looked* sound at the time — M10's two halves were separable, and the clock half needed no parts — but the separation only bought a day: it was written down as carrying ~450 of the ~640 ms, and when the clock half was actually measured on 2026-07-31 it carried **32**. Both halves are gone. The lesson that survives is to **cost procurement at the same time as arithmetic**, which was done for the camera ([M7b](milestones.md#m7b--source-the-camera--runs-in-parallel-it-exists-because-of-lead-time)) and not here; the lesson that got added is that *a fallback which has never been measured is not a mitigation*, it is a second unverified claim standing behind the first |

## The name

The project was called **MicroCLIP** from M9 until 2026-08-11. It is now
**fpga-open-vocab**. Three reasons, in the order they surfaced:

- **A collision.** [arXiv 2510.02270](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02270) is a
  paper called MicroCLIP. A side project does not get to keep a name a paper is
  already using.
- **It named the wrong model.** [M18](milestones.md) replaced the teacher —
  CLIP ViT-B/16 out, SigLIP 2 SO400M through a frozen PCA-512 in. A name that
  points at a model goes stale the next time the teacher changes, and this one
  already had. Naming it after the *new* teacher would only reset the clock.
- **It advertised "small" where the interesting claim is "arbitrary".**
  *Open-vocabulary* is the term of art for what the board actually does: you
  type a phrase nobody trained on and it answers. It names a **capability**
  rather than an architecture, so it survives another teacher swap, another
  chip, and another quantization.

Two families were considered and rejected for over-claiming. **`VLM`** and
**`multimodal`** both promise something the device does not have: the board
runs the **image tower only** — no tokenizer, no text encoder, no decoder, and
no token ever crosses the link. 512 floats do. The *system* is cross-modal; the
*board* is deliberately not, and evicting the language side to the host is the
reason 1.40 M parameters and 7,384 LE are enough. A name that hides that hides
the design. **`VLA`** is further off still: there is no action space and no
policy — the LED is a display, not a learned output.

`CLIP` and `multimodal` are still the words people search for, so they live in
the repository description and topics, where they cost nothing and claim
nothing. The rename itself touched 19 occurrences in 7 files.
