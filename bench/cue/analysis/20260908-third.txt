WHY IT WAS RE-RUN.  Until today the script enrolled the empty reference from
`empties[0]` and held out the rest, so the whole table was one draw of "which
empty span did the operator show first" - on exactly the reference #18 says
wanders.  The same bug had just been found in probe_multivisit.py, where it was
worth four points and a wrong conclusion.  Every arm now rotates over every
empty span, including the arms that never look at an empty reference, so the
comparison stays on one test set.

WHAT CHANGED.  Less than the multivisit case: three-nn 77.5 -> 78.2 pooled.  The
spread moved more than the mean - three-nn over shipped went from +23.3 at
t = 6.12 to +24.2 at t = 7.62 on the same 31 benches, and it now wins 27 of 31
rather than 26.  The single draw was noise, not bias.  The pre-rotation numbers
are not reproduced below, only summarised here, because they were a sample of
one enrolment each.

WHAT THE NEW ARM SAYS.  `three2` averages the empty reference over TWO spans -
the presence-stage twin of probe_multivisit.py's arm C.  It needs a third span
to hold out, so it is scored on 29 benches and paired against three-nn on those
29 only.  +0.3 points, t = 0.67 on 28 df, wins 12/29, worst -7.2.  That is a
coin flip, and unlike arm C it is not aimed at the benches that lose most.  #18
does not need a second '0' press.

The reason is in the drift column and not in the arm: steady benches score 84.1
and wandering ones 71.9, r = -0.405, and averaging two positions of a desk that
is moving gives a position the desk is not at either.  The remaining loss is
#30's, not this issue's.

`midpoint` stays dead: -23.0 against three-nn, wins 4/31, and `pos` has median
0.76 with 23 of 31 outside 0.25-0.75.  The empty desk is not in the middle, so
the '0' press cannot be skipped even though the second one is not worth making.
